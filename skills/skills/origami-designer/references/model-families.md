# Model-family heuristics

These are broad design families, not diagrams to copy.

## Dog

Beginner route: dog face. Start with a diagonal triangle, fold two top corners down for ears, fold bottom point for muzzle or chin, then draw eyes/nose after folding. Shape does most of the work; decoration should be post-fold unless surface mapping is explicitly traced.

## Hamster

Beginner route: round rodent face. Use blintz or animal-face logic to round corners into cheeks, create small ears, and expose a central face panel. A hamster is often more recognisable by cheeks, ears, small nose, and warm colour than by full body shape.

## Flower

Beginner route: tulip or blintz flower. Petals can be made by folded corners. Flowers tolerate symbolic geometry well because radial or bilateral petals read clearly.

## Bus

Beginner route: flat symbolic vehicle. Fold a square into a wide rectangle or envelope-like body. The fold creates a stable rectangular silhouette; the bus identity comes from windows high, wheels low, and a clear front. Prefer post-fold drawing, or pre-map windows and wheels only after tracing visible panels.

## Dress

Beginner route: dress silhouette. Use cupboard/kite logic to form a bodice and flared skirt. Surface decoration is plausible because clothing is patterned, but ensure patterns land on visible skirt/body panels.

## Doll or person

Beginner route: simple person, kimono-like figure, or dress form with head panel. Avoid overclaiming anatomical detail. Use face/headscarf/body panels and post-fold drawing for features.

## Ukrainian doll

Prefer a motanka or vyshyvanka-inspired paper doll rather than a nesting doll. Fold topology supplies head/body/dress panels. Surface identity can use headscarf, central apron, red/black embroidery motifs, and blue/yellow accents. State that cultural identity is carried mainly by visible costume design.
