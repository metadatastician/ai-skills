# Surface mapping

Printable origami decoration is an inverse mapping problem.

## Rule

Final visible feature -> final folded panel -> unfolded square region -> printed artwork.

If the path is unknown, do not pre-print the feature. Add it after folding.

## Failure examples

- Dog bones printed randomly on the square do not make a dog.
- Bus wheels printed in the middle of a hidden flap do not make a bus.
- A yellow triangle cannot fold into a brown circle unless the brown circle exists on a revealed surface or the result is an approximation.
- Themed background patterns are decorative paper, not fold instructions.

## Practical strategy

1. Decide the final viewing side.
2. Mark visible final panels.
3. Trace those panels back to the square.
4. Place only essential features there.
5. Keep fold marks legible.
6. Use post-fold drawing for eyes, wheels, windows, embroidery, or faces when the mapping is uncertain.

## Disclosure labels

- Shape-led: recognisable from folds alone.
- Surface-led: recognisable mainly from drawn/printed features.
- Hybrid: folds create the silhouette; drawn/printed features disambiguate.
