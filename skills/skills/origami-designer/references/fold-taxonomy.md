# Fold taxonomy

Use this as a compact vocabulary reference.

## Primitive folds

- Valley fold: bring the paper toward the folder; the crease valley opens upward in diagrams.
- Mountain fold: fold away from the folder; the crease forms a ridge.
- Unfold: open the paper after setting a crease.
- Turn over: flip the sheet to work on the other side.
- Rotate: change page orientation without changing layer order.

## Common compound folds

- Inside reverse fold: open a flap and invert a point inward along existing creases.
- Outside reverse fold: invert a point outward around existing creases.
- Squash fold: open a pocket and flatten it symmetrically.
- Pleat: adjacent mountain and valley folds forming a step.
- Crimp: local pleated displacement, often for heads, beaks, tails, or vehicle fronts.
- Rabbit ear: two valley folds and one mountain fold gather a triangular flap into a point.
- Petal fold: lift a flap while bringing edges to a centre line; common in bird/frog bases.
- Sink fold: push a point inside the model; advanced for beginners unless shown clearly.
- Blintz fold: fold corners to the centre.
- Cupboard fold: fold left and right edges to the centre line.

## Bases and families

- Kite base: two adjacent edges folded to a diagonal; useful for heads, ears, tails, dresses.
- Preliminary/square base: collapse from diagonal and horizontal/vertical creases; used in many animals and flowers.
- Waterbomb base: triangular collapsed base; useful for inflatable or petal forms.
- Fish base: elongated base from kite/preliminary logic; useful for animals.
- Bird base: preliminary base plus petal folds; cranes and more advanced animals.
- Frog base: bird-base extension; advanced for beginners.

## Instruction discipline

Use standard vocabulary, but do not hide complexity behind it. If a fold is likely hard for a beginner, give an intermediate diagram or choose a simpler model.
