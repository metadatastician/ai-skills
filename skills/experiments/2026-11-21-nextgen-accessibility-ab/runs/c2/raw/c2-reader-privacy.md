### F1. Choosing Easy Read is a separate server request that implies a need
- Where: `dosing.html` link `<a href="dosing-easy-read.html">Easy Read version</a>`; `dosing-easy-read.html` as a whole, plus its six symbol images (`sym/medicine.png`, `sym/spoon.png`, `sym/baby.png`, `sym/clock.png`, `sym/bottle.png`, `sym/doctor.png`), which only this page loads.
- Problem: The only way to get the adapted form is to fetch a different URL from the publisher. That fetch, and the six image fetches that come with it, put a line in the trust's server, CDN or proxy logs, with IP address, user agent, timestamp and any cookies. Easy Read is commonly used by people with learning disabilities, so a log entry for `dosing-easy-read.html` implies a need, even though some readers choose it for other reasons. The choice of form happens on the publisher's side, not the reader's. This breaks the theory's rule that no request may reveal a reader's needs.
- Who is affected: Every reader who opens the Easy Read version, including people with learning disabilities, people with low literacy, people reading in a second language, and carers reading for someone else.
- Impact: The trust holds data that implies a need and is tied to an IP address and possibly an identity, though it never asked for it and does not need it. Assumption, not advice: under UK GDPR, data that implies a disability may count as special category data. If so, the trust needs an Article 9 condition to process it, and retention and access controls must cover the logs. (ASSUMED.)
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Smallest fix: send both versions to every reader in one document, and let the reader switch between them with a toggle that runs only on the device. Do not change the URL, set a cookie, send a request or fire an analytics event on the switch. Keep the choice in local storage at most. Load the symbol images for everyone, or inline them, so the image fetches reveal nothing. If a separate URL has to stay, do not log the path with client identifiers, do not set cookies on either page, and keep its traffic out of any analytics.
- Evidence: Both files as supplied. Neither file shows any analytics, cookies or server configuration, so the log exposure is inferred from how ordinary web serving works. It is not observed.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test whether an in-page toggle is as easy to find and use as a separate link, for Easy Read users and for screen-reader users, and whether switching keeps their place on the page.

### F2. Easy Read plus a child-medicine topic builds a sensitive profile (the composition leak)
- Where: The log entries from F1, combined with the page topic (paracetamol dosing for children, including babies) and the time of the request.
- Problem: Each signal is harmless on its own: a request for a children's medicine page, a request for an Easy Read page, an IP address, a time of day. Together they say that a person at this address, who may have a learning disability, is caring for a sick young child, possibly a baby, possibly at night. This is the composition leak the theory describes.
- Who is affected: Parents and carers who may have learning disabilities, and their children.
- Impact: If the trust's logs are disclosed, breached, matched with patient records or reached through data sharing, they could support inferences about a parent's capacity to care for a child. That harm is serious and hard to undo. Assumption, not advice: the combination probably raises both the special category issue and the duty of care beyond what either signal raises alone. (ASSUMED. Nothing in the case shows the harm has happened.)
- Severity: 2
- Fix: The F1 fix removes the Easy Read signal, so the topic request is all that remains. Also apply short log retention, strip or truncate IP addresses, and do not link web logs to patient or service records.
- Evidence: The page topic and audience are as given in the case. Log contents and retention are not shown, so the profile is inferred.
- Needs disabled readers: no

### F3. Referrer headers carry the Easy Read path onward
- Where: Every link in `dosing-easy-read.html`: `/` (header), `dosing.html` ("Back to the full page") and `/accessibility` (footer). Neither file sets a `Referrer-Policy` or `<meta name="referrer">`.
- Problem: With the browser's default policy (`strict-origin-when-cross-origin`), same-origin navigations send the full referrer. Every page a reader visits from the Easy Read page therefore receives `dosing-easy-read.html` as the referrer. The need signal spreads to more log lines and to any page-level analytics on those pages. All links are same-origin today, so no third party receives it. A cross-origin link added later would receive only the origin under the default policy.
- Who is affected: Easy Read readers who go on to any other page.
- Impact: The need signal is repeated and becomes easier to join with the reader's later activity, for example the accessibility statement or other pages on the site.
- Severity: 1
- Fix: Set `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` or `same-origin`, with path stripping enforced on the server, for the Easy Read page. The F1 fix makes this unnecessary, because the reader would have no Easy Read URL to leak.
- Evidence: Markup as supplied. The browser default is standard behaviour. Server headers are not shown.
- Needs disabled readers: no

### F4. The Easy Read version leaves out the dose, so getting it in an accessible form means asking a person
- Where: `dosing-easy-read.html`: "Look at the box to see how much to give." and "Give your child 1 dose every 4 hours." It has no dose table, no "no more than 4 doses in 24 hours" limit, no warning about other medicines that contain paracetamol, and no "call 999 if too much". It says "under 3 months" where the current original says "under 2 months". It was made on 14 March 2026, before the original's 3 November 2026 correction.
- Problem: From the privacy lens: a reader who needs the dose in an accessible form cannot get it on their own device. The adapted form sends them to the box text, which is the inaccessible form, or to "talk to a doctor". To get the substance, they have to go to a person and, in effect, disclose that they need help. The theory requires the reader to choose the form while the record fixes the substance. Here the form they chose loses the substance, and the only way back costs them their privacy. The fidelity faults are for the fidelity reviewers. Under the theory they would make this version Unfaithful, so it should be withheld. That is a reading of the supplied text, not a check against a declaration, because none is supplied.
- Who is affected: Easy Read readers who are giving a child medicine.
- Impact: The reader either discloses or doses without the limits. The missing 24-hour limit and the missing overdose instruction are a safety risk for the child.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Withhold this version until it is remade from the current original, and checked, with every critical dosing item kept, including the dose by age in an Easy Read table. Do this together with the F1 fix, so a reader can get the dose without asking anyone and without sending a signal.
- Evidence: The two files compared side by side. The rating rests on the theory's outcome rules, not on a tested claim. Whether AI adaptations break declared items at material rates is OPEN.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Easy Read users should decide whether a remade version showing the dose by age is intact and usable, and whether they can follow it without help.

Both pages size text in `rem` or leave it unset, so browser zoom, font and colour overrides work on the device and send nothing.
