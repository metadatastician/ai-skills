### F1. A visit to the accessibility statement can be joined to a named sign-up in server logs
- Where: `signup.html` footer link `<a href="/accessibility">`, read alongside the form's `action="/newsletter" method="post"` with fields `fname` and `email`.
- Problem: Each request is ordinary, so neither one reveals a need by itself. But a GET for `/accessibility`, then a POST to `/newsletter` from the same IP address, User-Agent and session, joins an act that hints at a need (reading the accessibility statement) to a first name and email address. The same is true of the `Referer` header if the reader goes from `/accessibility` back to the form and submits it. This is a weak, indirect inference. It only becomes a risk if logs are kept and joined, or if per-visitor analytics run on these pages. The case material does not show either.
- Who is affected: Readers who open the accessibility statement before or after signing up, and who can then be named through the email address they gave.
- Impact: If the publisher or a log processor joins these records, it holds an inference about a named person's possible disability. That could count as special category data under UK GDPR (ASSUMED, not advice; counsel's opinion pending). The inference is also unreliable, because many people read accessibility statements for other reasons.
- Severity: 1
- Fix: Keep request logs short-lived and do not join them to subscriber records. Run no per-visitor analytics or beacons on `/accessibility`. Send `Referrer-Policy: same-origin` or `no-referrer` so the statement page is not carried forward as a referrer. Add no tracking parameters to the footer link.
- Evidence: The two links and the form `action` in `signup.html`. No logging, analytics or cookie code appears in the file. The server side was not supplied, so whether logs exist or are joined is unknown.
- Needs disabled readers: no

### F2. The newsletter offers no neutral format choice, so a future accessible-format route could force disclosure
- Where: The sign-up form (`fname`, `email` only) and the intro text "One email a month…". The newsletter is the adapted-form path the reader actually receives.
- Problem: The form offers no format choice for the newsletter, such as plain text, HTML or larger text. A reader who needs a different form now has only one route left: contacting the charity, probably through the accessibility statement. That kind of request usually asks the reader to name a need. If the charity later adds a field to the form, it may be worded as a need ("I need an accessible version") rather than a preference. Either way, a disability declaration would be stored against a named subscriber record. The page as supplied does not do this. The finding is about the absence of a neutral route and the likely shape of the route that would replace it.
- Who is affected: Subscribers who need the newsletter in another form.
- Impact: The reader has to disclose a need to get an accessible form, which the theory forbids. The publisher would then hold special category data tied to a name and email address (ASSUMED, not advice).
- Severity: 1
- Fix: If a format option is added, offer it to everyone as a neutral preference ("Plain text email" / "Formatted email"), with no reason asked and no wording about need. Better still, send a well-formed multipart email (a real plain-text part plus semantic HTML) so the reader's own mail client does the adapting on their device and nothing about them is stored.
- Evidence: The form fields and intro paragraph in `signup.html`. The `/accessibility` contents were not supplied.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test whether the neutral choices (plain text or multipart HTML) give them the newsletter in a usable form without asking them for anything, and whether any wording on the form reads to them as asking for a disclosure.

Not found: on-page adaptation controls of any kind (font, contrast, motion, simplify, Easy Read, read-aloud, "screen reader mode"), so no adaptation signal leaves the device; the page relies on the browser and the reader's own settings. Also not found: URL parameters, cookies, `localStorage`, analytics or beacon calls, third-party scripts or fonts, and assistive technology detection (no `matchMedia`, user-agent sniffing or focus or keyboard heuristics). Client-side validation sends nothing: the error message, `aria-invalid` and the `#email` fragment link all stay on the device. No form field names or implies a need, and the submitted fields (`fname`, `email`) do not combine into a needs profile. No required declaration of purpose was found. Not supplied and therefore not assessed: the server handler for `/newsletter`, server-side error responses, log retention, the contents of `/privacy` and `/accessibility`, and tracking pixels or link tracking in the newsletter emails themselves.
