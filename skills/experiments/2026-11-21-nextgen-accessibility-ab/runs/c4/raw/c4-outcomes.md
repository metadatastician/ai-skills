Reader tasks this material exists for: (a) choose reading settings knowing what each one does and who learns of it; (b) read a book chapter, and its notes, correctly through the path those settings select; (c) trust that a change of settings took effect, on every device. Paths walked: the settings panel read visually and in screen-reader order; the original-text chapter; the "Simplified text" chapter; the "screen reader mode" chapter; the edge, analytics and account record that the settings feed.

### F1. Simplified text is an unlabelled, unchecked machine rewrite that readers will take for the book
- Where: integration.md bullet 2 (`level=simplified`); settings.html Reading level fieldset ("Simplified text (rewritten for easier reading)").
- Problem: When a reader chooses Simplified, Lumen Edge sends the chapter to its own rewriting model and returns the result with no marker. No must-survive declaration exists, nobody checks the output, and the reader is not told the text is machine-made or unchecked. Under the theory this version can carry at most the label Unchecked. Nothing shows that label or offers the original alongside it, and no Withheld path exists for passages where a critical item (a direct quote, a number, a hedge, who said what) would break. The publisher is not told. The rewrite is made from the original chapter, so this is not a chain fault. The fault is that the version is unlabelled and unchecked.
- Who is affected: Readers who choose Simplified, most likely readers with cognitive or learning disabilities, aphasia or low literacy, and readers using a second language. These readers are the most likely to rely on the rewrite alone and least likely to check it against the original.
- Impact: Performance: a reader may finish a chapter believing they read the book's content when they read a model's paraphrase of it. Safety: in non-fiction (health, law, money, history) a changed number, a dropped hedge or a misattributed quote misinforms, and the reader cannot detect it. Comfort: checking means switching the account-wide setting and reloading, which is effort these readers should not have to spend. How often AI adaptations break declared items is OPEN in the evidence table, and so is whether labels improve outcomes. The missing label and the missing check follow directly from the integration text. The EU AI Act Art. 50 duty to mark machine-made substantive rewrites (from 2 August 2026) also appears unmet. That is a legal reading, ASSUMED.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Mark every simplified chapter at the point of reading as "Simplified by Lumen Edge's AI model, not checked against the original", with the date it was made and a one-step link to the original passage. Show Checked only when a check against a declaration has been done and passed. For works whose publisher supplies a declaration, withhold rewritten passages that would break a critical item, and say they were withheld. In the panel, say plainly that the rewrite is made by a machine and is unchecked.
- Evidence: integration.md: "returns the rewritten chapter. The original publisher is not told, and the rewritten text carries no marker." settings.html label text gives no hint that the rewrite is automated or unchecked.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with readers who would choose Simplified. After each reads a simplified non-fiction chapter: Who said the quoted remark? What is the figure given for X? Did the author say X is certain or only likely? Was what you read the author's own words? Score each answer as correctly informed, uninformed or misinformed, against readers of the original. What "intact" means for these readers' versions is for them to decide (principle 5), not the reviewer.

### F2. "Screen reader mode" removes footnotes and publisher's notes without telling the reader
- Where: integration.md bullet 3 (`sr=1`); settings.html Screen reader mode fieldset ("I use a screen reader").
- Problem: Ticking the box makes Lumen Edge strip images and "decorative" sidebars, and footnotes and publisher's notes count as decorative. That content is not decoration. Footnotes carry sources, caveats and corrections, and publisher's notes carry context and errata. Nothing tells the reader content was removed, and image content is removed rather than described. Measured against a full-text version, this is a set of silent drops, some of them potentially critical items (corrections, caveats). Under the theory such a version is Unfaithful and should not be offered.
- Who is affected: Blind and low-vision screen-reader users, and anyone else who ticks the box, for example people with reading or cognitive disabilities who use read-aloud. These readers rely on this version exclusively and have no visual cue that footnote markers or figures have gone.
- Impact: Performance: readers get less of the book than sighted readers and cannot follow references or figure-based arguments. Safety: they may read a claim without its caveat or correction and act on it, for example in a health or study text. Comfort: anyone who finds out has to untick the box and reload to get the notes back, and then loses whatever the mode was meant to help with.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Do not remove content because someone uses a screen reader. Keep footnotes and notes as linked, navigable elements, and keep images with their text alternatives. If any declutter option remains, it should be a separate, plainly named choice that lists what it hides, and it should never hide corrections or caveats. When anything is hidden, say so at the point of reading.
- Evidence: integration.md: "removes images and 'decorative' sidebars, including footnotes and the publisher's notes". The panel label says only "I use a screen reader".
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with screen-reader users reading a chapter that has footnoted caveats and a figure, with the mode on and with it off: What source supports claim X? Is claim X qualified anywhere? What does figure N show? Were any parts of the chapter left out? Readers using the mode should also say whether any declutter is wanted at all, and what it must never remove.

### F3. Settings presented as look-and-sound choices change content and disclose needs
- Where: settings.html intro ("Choose how books look and sound") and the Reading level and Screen reader mode labels.
- Problem: The task here is an informed choice. The panel frames every option as presentation, but two options change what the reader receives (F1, F2) and all of them are sent to third parties (F4). Nothing on the panel says who rewrites the text, that notes are removed, or who receives the settings. The single "Privacy" link in the footer is the only route to that information, and the panel gives no reason to follow it.
- Who is affected: Every reader who uses the panel. Exposure is highest for people with cognitive disabilities, who are less likely to follow a footer link to a privacy policy, and for screen-reader users, who never reach the footer before submitting.
- Impact: Performance: readers choose without knowing the consequences. Safety: they are misinformed about what they will receive and who will learn of their needs. Comfort: the information is spread across pages they are unlikely to open.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Next to each option, say in a short line what actually happens. Examples: "An AI model run by Lumen Edge rewrites the text; it is not checked." "Your settings are sent to Lumen Edge." Change the intro so it does not promise look-and-sound only. Better still, fix F1, F2 and F4 so that the reassuring description becomes true.
- Evidence: settings.html line "Choose how books look and sound. Your settings are saved to your library account and follow you to any device." compared with integration.md bullets 1–4.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with panel users before and after a choice: What will change in your book if you choose this? Who, besides you, will know which settings you chose? Score correct, don't know or wrong.

### F4. Reading settings are sent to a vendor's analytics as disability segments
- Where: integration.md bullets 1, 4 and 5. settings.html script writes the `rp` cookie for a year, and the header shows "Signed in as reader 20481193".
- Problem: On every request the settings are sent, as `X-Reader-Prefs`, to the origin and to Lumen Edge analytics, together with the reader's IP address and user agent. Analytics turns them into labelled disability segments ("Accessibility: low vision", "Accessibility: cognitive") that the library can view, and the account API keys the same settings to the library card number. These are needs disclosed through a header and analytics, which is special category data under UK GDPR (ASSUMED). Combining settings with IP and user agent creates the composition leak. Size, contrast, spacing and font could be applied on the reader's own device and need not leave it.
- Who is affected: Every reader who changes a setting. The most exposed are readers whose combination is rare, who become identifiable, and readers who would avoid the settings if they knew. Those readers then lose the adaptation they need.
- Impact: Performance: a reader who learns this may stop using settings they need. Safety: the reader is misinformed about who learns of their disability, and the profile is out of their control. Comfort: the only way to protect their privacy is to read without adaptations. The card number in the page header can also be seen or read aloud on shared or public devices.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Apply presentation settings on the device and do not send them in headers or to analytics. Remove the disability segments. If cross-device sync is kept, store the settings so that only the reader's own devices can read them, not keyed to the card number in clear and not visible to the vendor. If simplification is still done by an intermediary, ask for it per request without sending any other setting with it. Mask the card number in the header.
- Evidence: integration.md: "forwards it, with the reader's IP address and user agent, to the library origin and to Lumen Edge analytics"; "builds 'reader segments' ... 'Accessibility: low vision' ... 'Accessibility: cognitive'"; "stores the same settings against the library card number". settings.html: `max-age=31536000` cookie and "Signed in as reader 20481193".
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Ask readers who would use the settings, after they have read a plain statement of the data flow: Would you still turn these settings on? Which ones would you avoid? What would you expect the library to know about you? Their answers decide whether the privacy design affects whether they use adaptations. The reviewer should not assume the answer.

### F5. "Settings saved." is shown even when nothing was saved
- Where: settings.html script, submit handler.
- Problem: The handler does not check `response.ok`, so an HTTP error from the PUT still displays "Settings saved.". If the network fails, `fetch` rejects, nothing is shown, and the reader gets no message at all. Either way the status region can mislead or stay silent.
- Who is affected: Every reader. Screen-reader users depend on the `role="status"` announcement because they have no visual confirmation, and readers with memory or cognitive disabilities depend on clear confirmation.
- Impact: Safety: a reader who turned Simplified or screen reader mode off may believe it is off on all devices when it is not, and then reads a rewritten or stripped book thinking it is the original. Performance: the promise that settings "follow you to any device" fails without any sign. Comfort: the only way to find the failure is to try again on another device.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Check the response. Report failure in the status region in plain words, keep the reader's choices on screen and offer to retry. Write the cookie only after the save succeeds, or state that the setting applies on this device only.
- Evidence: `await fetch(...)` result is discarded; `status.textContent = 'Settings saved.'` runs unconditionally after it.
- Needs disabled readers: no. This is a defect that can be shown by forcing a failed save.

### F6. Unticking a checkbox may not turn the mode off
- Where: settings.html script, `Object.fromEntries(new FormData(e.target))`.
- Problem: FormData leaves out unchecked checkboxes. Unticking "I use a screen reader" or "Reduce animation" therefore sends no `sr` or `motion` key at all. It does not send an "off" value. Whether the account API and Lumen Edge treat a missing key as off, or keep the stored value, is not shown in the material. If they merge, the reader cannot leave screen reader mode, and keeps losing footnotes (F2).
- Who is affected: Readers who tried a mode and want to stop it, including people who ticked "I use a screen reader" to see what it does.
- Impact: Safety: the reader keeps receiving altered content while being told "Settings saved.". Performance: the switch does not do what it says. This depends on server behaviour that the material does not show.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Send an explicit value for every setting (for example `sr=0`, `motion=none`). Make the API replace the whole record. After saving, read the stored settings back and show them.
- Evidence: Checkbox inputs `name="motion"` and `name="sr"` have no hidden counterpart, and FormData drops unchecked boxes. The merge or replace behaviour of the API is not in the case material.
- Needs disabled readers: no. Verify by unticking, saving and inspecting the stored record and the header.

Not found: any view of the reading page itself, so whether the active version (original, simplified, screen reader mode) is shown where the book is read could not be checked; any must-survive declaration from publishers; any check, label or Withheld path for Lumen Edge output; any per-request way to switch to the original; the content of the "/privacy" and "/accessibility" pages; any statement of how the account API handles missing keys; any involvement of disabled readers in defining these modes.
