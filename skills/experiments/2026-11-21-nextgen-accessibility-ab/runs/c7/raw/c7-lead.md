### F1. AI summary reverses Lindqvist's position, invents agreement and drops his alternative
- Where: lecture.html, `section.ai`, sentence 1 ("Both speakers agreed that the four-day school week has failed and should be scrapped."), and the summary as a whole.
- Problem: The source has two opposed positions. Okafor: "I think it should be scrapped." Lindqvist: "I disagree that it should be scrapped." and "My view is: reform it. Keep the four-day week for older pupils, and fund Friday provision for the youngest." The chair: "So, not agreement, but some common ground on secondary schools." The summary says the reverse: both agreed. Lindqvist's position is changed and misattributed, the two positions are merged, and "has failed" is an evaluative claim neither speaker made. Lindqvist's reform proposal and Okafor's reply ("I could live with that for secondary schools. I'm not there for primary.") are absent, and no cut is named. Changing, misattributing and merging are distortion whatever an item's weight, so the class is Unfaithful and the only allowed state is Withheld. The weights the reviewers gave the dropped items (critical, important) are the team's own derivation; there is no author declaration, so those weights are provisional. The Unfaithful class does not depend on them.
- Who is affected: Every reader who takes the summary as the gist rather than the 58-minute video or the collapsed transcript. Readers who rely on short text to reduce reading or listening load are the most likely to rely on it alone. It also misrepresents a named academic's public position.
- Impact: Readers are told the opposite of the debate's central fact: who held which view, and that there was no agreement.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Withhold the summary now. Any replacement must be made from the edited transcript (F7), checked against an author's must-survive declaration that marks each speaker's position, Lindqvist's reform proposal, Okafor's secondary/primary reply and the chair's "not agreement", and labelled Unchecked until that check is done.
- Evidence: Summary sentence 1 against transcript paragraphs 2 to 5 and caption cues 00:03:12.000 and 00:04:40.000. The contradiction is direct in the text.
- Needs disabled readers: No, to establish the distortion. Yes, for any replacement: what counts as "intact" for a debate summary must be settled with the readers it is made for (principle 5). Test summary-only readers against transcript readers with questions such as "Did the two speakers agree on whether the four-day week should be scrapped?" and "What did Professor Lindqvist propose instead?", coding each answer as correctly informed, uninformed or misinformed.

### F2. AI summary invents a policy decision and attributes it to the chair
- Where: lecture.html, `section.ai`, sentence 3 ("The chair concluded that the policy would be reversed next year.").
- Problem: The chair said: "The evaluation report is due next spring, and the minister has said nothing will change before it is published." The summary adds a claim the source does not support (reversal, next year), gives it to the chair, and contradicts what the chair reports of the minister. The real status, nothing changes before the report, is dropped. A new factual claim makes the version Unfaithful.
- Who is affected: Every summary reader, including parents and staff in the pilot districts who may plan around policy.
- Impact: Readers are given a false fact about future government action under a named person's authority, and could act on it.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Withhold the summary (F1). In a replacement, keep the chair's statement of the status quo and attribute "nothing will change" to the minister, as reported by the chair. Log any added claim about outcomes or timing separately and withhold any version that contains one.
- Evidence: Summary sentence 3 against transcript paragraph 5.
- Needs disabled readers: No, to establish the defect. For a replacement, include "Has a decision been made about the four-day week, and who said so?" in the F1 outcome test.

### F3. The 12 per cent figure loses its hedge and scope and is given to both speakers
- Where: lecture.html, `section.ai`, sentence 2 ("Dr Okafor and Professor Lindqvist cited falling attainment, with test scores down 12%").
- Problem: Okafor said "In the pilot districts, reading scores for the youngest pupils are down by about 12 per cent". The summary drops "about", widens "reading scores for the youngest pupils" to "test scores" and "falling attainment", and attributes the figure to both speakers. Lindqvist accepted the reading figure ("I accept the reading figures for the youngest pupils") but gave counter-evidence ("maths scores are flat, attendance is up, and teacher vacancies in those districts have halved"), which the summary drops without naming the cut. The widening is false on the source: maths scores are "flat".
- Who is affected: Every summary reader, especially anyone who repeats or acts on the number, and anyone hearing it read aloud without the transcript beside it.
- Impact: A hedged, narrowly scoped claim by one speaker becomes a certain, general claim both speakers endorse.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: In any replacement keep "about 12 per cent", "reading", "youngest pupils" and "pilot districts" together and attributed to Okafor; state that Lindqvist accepted the reading figure, and keep his three counter-figures or name the cut.
- Evidence: Caption cues 00:03:16.500 and 00:04:44.000 to 00:04:53.000, transcript paragraphs 2 and 3, against summary sentence 2.
- Needs disabled readers: No, to establish the defect. For a replacement, add "What fell by about 12 per cent, for which pupils, and who gave the figure?" to the F1 outcome test.

### F5. The summary's label states machine provenance only: no check status, no cuts, and its source line is the tool's own claim

It comes after the summary text in reading order and is set smaller, so readers meet the claims before the caveat. A machine-made mark of the EU AI Act Article 50 kind says a machine was involved, not what changed; whether this mark satisfies Article 50 was not assessed (legal points ASSUMED). Severity is 2, not the 3 one reviewer gave: the harm of the false content is already counted in F1 to F4, and this finding covers the missing status.
- Who is affected: Every summary reader, especially those who stop after the summary.
- Impact: The mark passes as disclosure while giving readers no basis to judge the summary, and no warning that it contradicts the transcript below it.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Show the current summary as Withheld with a reason. For any replacement, put the label before the text ("Unchecked machine summary: may contain errors; the transcript below is the record"), and change it to Checked only after a check against the declaration, with important cuts named. Never upgrade the label automatically.
- Evidence: The heading, the `p.meta` text, its DOM position after the summary paragraph, and `.ai p.meta { font-size: .9rem; }`. The meta line's contrast (#3d3d3d on #f4f4f4) is not a fault; the issue is order and content.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test whether readers notice a label placed before or after the summary, and whether it changes how far they rely on it. Whether labels improve outcomes is OPEN, so a label must not be treated as the fix on its own.

### F6. The page leads readers to the summary and hides the transcript
- Where: lecture.html. `section.ai` is a named region with the page's only `h2`, placed before `<details><summary>Full transcript</summary>`, which has no heading and starts collapsed.
- Problem: Heading and landmark navigation reach the summary but not the transcript, and visually the summary is open and boxed while the transcript is closed. The shortest path to the content leads to the least faithful version. That this burdens people more than software rests on a DESIGNED, untested claim. Severity is 2, not 3: the structural fault is real on its own, but its worst effect is the content harm already counted in F1 to F4. One reviewer's statement that "automated checks pass both" was not run and is struck.
- Who is affected: Screen-reader users navigating by heading or landmark, readers steered to the shortest version to save effort, and Deaf and hard-of-hearing readers who want text and must find and expand the transcript.
- Impact: Getting the faithful account takes extra effort; getting the misleading one takes none.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Give the transcript its own `h2`, and open it by default or link to it next to the video. Make the record at least as easy to reach as any adaptation.
- Evidence: Heading structure and DOM order in lecture.html.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test which version screen-reader users, readers with cognitive disabilities and Deaf readers reach first, and how long it takes them to reach the transcript; then run the F1 questions by the path each person actually took.

### F9. Speaker names in the captions exist only as WebVTT voice tags

- Severity: 2
- Fix: Check rendering in the target browsers. If names are not shown, add visible speaker labels at each change of speaker.
- Evidence: Cue text in captions-excerpt.vtt. Rendering is untested.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with caption users, in the actual player, whether they can tell who is speaking at each change.

### F10. Getting audio description requires a request that reveals the need
- Where: lecture.html, `<a href="audio-described.mp4">Version with audio description</a>`.
- Problem: Audio description is available only as a separate file that only readers who want description will request. The path, IP address, time and referrer reach the University's server or CDN, which breaks principle 4. The page shows no logging or analytics, so whether these requests are logged, kept or linked to identity is not known. If they are, the data is likely special category data under UK GDPR (ASSUMED, counsel's opinion pending).
- Who is affected: Blind and partially sighted readers, and anyone else who uses description.
- Impact: The publisher, or whoever runs its servers and logs, can learn who needed description.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Deliver description in the media request every reader makes, for example as an alternate audio rendition in one adaptive stream chosen on the device. If a separate file must stay, exclude its path from analytics, keep no per-IP or per-cookie access logs for it, keep retention short and send no referrer; this reduces the leak but does not remove it.
- Evidence: The anchor in lecture.html. The logging risk is inferred from the request's shape, not observed.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with blind and partially sighted readers whether in-stream description works in their own players and screen readers as well as a separate file does.
