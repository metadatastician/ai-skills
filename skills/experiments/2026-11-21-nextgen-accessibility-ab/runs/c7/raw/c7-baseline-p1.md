### F1. The AI summary gets the debate's outcome wrong
- Where: lecture.html, `section.ai`, first `<p>` (line 129 of the case file)
- Problem: The summary says "Both speakers agreed that the four-day school week has failed and should be scrapped." The transcript and captions show the opposite. Lindqvist says "I disagree that it should be scrapped ... My view is: reform it", and the chair closes with "So, not agreement, but some common ground on secondary schools." The summary also says "The chair concluded that the policy would be reversed next year." The chair actually said "the minister has said nothing will change before [the evaluation report] is published." The summary also attributes "parents had been left without childcare on Fridays" to both speakers, when only Okafor said it.
- Who is affected: People who read the summary instead of watching or reading the full text. These include people with cognitive or learning disabilities, people with fatigue or attention-related conditions, screen-reader users who want to avoid a long transcript, and Deaf users for whom English is a second language. COGA guidance tells these readers to rely on summaries.
- Impact: These readers come away with the opposite of what was said: a consensus and a policy decision that never happened. They have no easy way to find out, because the correct account sits in a collapsed transcript. Some may repeat this as fact.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Remove the machine summary. Replace it with a short summary written or checked by a person against the edited transcript, for example: "The speakers disagreed. Dr Okafor wants the four-day week scrapped: reading scores for the youngest pupils are down about 12 per cent. Professor Lindqvist wants it reformed, not scrapped. They found some common ground on secondary schools. Nothing will change before the evaluation report next spring." Name who checked it and when. If automated summaries are kept anywhere, have a person approve each one before it is published.
- Evidence: Compare line 129 with lines 136–139 and with the caption cues at 00:04:40–00:04:53.
- Needs user testing: yes. Test whether readers who use only the summary can state each speaker's position and the outcome correctly.

### F2. The AI summary distorts the key statistic
- Where: lecture.html, `section.ai`, first `<p>`
- Problem: The summary says "falling attainment, with test scores down 12%". The source says "reading scores for the youngest pupils are down by about 12 per cent". It also says "maths scores are flat". The summary drops the subject (reading), the group (youngest pupils) and the hedge ("about"), and it turns one speaker's evidence into a shared claim.
- Who is affected: Readers who rely on the summary, as in F1, especially people with dyscalculia or limited numeracy, who are least able to cross-check a figure.
- Impact: Readers believe all test scores fell by exactly 12%, which neither speaker said.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Quote the figure as spoken, with its scope ("reading scores for the youngest pupils, down about 12 per cent"), and say who said it. Include the counter-evidence as well.
- Evidence: Line 129 against line 136 and caption cue 00:03:16.500.
- Needs user testing: no

### F3. The summary was made from the uncorrected automatic captions, and the page does not say what that means
- Where: lecture.html, `p.meta` ("Generated by StreamScribe from the automatic captions on 3 October 2026."), and line 125 ("Captions edited by the University's media team on 5 October 2026.")
- Problem: The summary was generated from automatic captions two days before the captions were corrected, so it rests on a source the university itself judged needed editing. The provenance note appears only after the summary, so screen-reader and linear readers take in the claims before the caveat. The note also does not say that the summary may be wrong or has not been checked. The heading "AI summary" gives no warning about reliability, and there is no way to report an error.
- Who is affected: Screen-reader users who read in order, people with cognitive disabilities who may not infer that "automatic captions" means "possibly inaccurate", and everyone who relies on the summary.
- Impact: The page presents misleading content as an equal alternative to the edited captions and transcript.
- Severity: 3. This is the cause of F1 and F2.
- Fix: Do not publish summaries generated from unedited automatic captions. If any summary remains, put a plain-language status before it ("Checked by [team] against the edited transcript on [date]"), and add a "Report a mistake" link.
- Evidence: Line 130 and line 125: the summary is dated 3 October and the caption edit 5 October.
- Needs user testing: no

### F4. The page gives the unreliable summary more prominence than the accurate transcript
- Where: lecture.html. `section.ai` is open and has an `<h2>`. The transcript is in a closed `<details>` with no heading.
- Problem: The AI summary is the only visible text alternative and the only `<h2>` on the page. "Full transcript" is collapsed and has no heading, so it does not appear in a screen reader's headings list.
- Who is affected: Screen-reader users who navigate by heading (H key, rotor), keyboard users, Deaf and hard-of-hearing users looking for the text version, and people with low digital confidence who may not know that a disclosure widget can be opened.
- Impact: Users are steered to the wrong content and may not find the transcript at all.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Give the transcript an `<h2>Transcript</h2>` and place it before any summary. Show it expanded, or keep the `<details>` but put it under the heading. Put any checked summary after the transcript heading, or under its own heading lower down the page.
- Evidence: Lines 127–140.
- Needs user testing: yes. Test whether screen-reader and cognitive-disability participants can find the transcript, and which text they use.

### F5. The transcript labelled "Full" is not the full 58-minute debate

- Problem: The transcript has about 220 words, while a 58-minute debate would run to roughly 7,000–9,000 words. It has no timestamps, and nothing says it has been abridged. - Severity: 2 (3 if the published transcript really is this short)

### F6. Caption viewers are not shown who is speaking
- Where: captions-excerpt.vtt, every cue, for example `<v Amara Okafor>I think it should be scrapped.`
- Problem: Speakers are identified only by WebVTT voice tags. Mainstream browser players (Chrome, Firefox, Safari) and many platform players do not show the voice name, so the cue appears without a speaker. In a debate where both speakers say "it should be scrapped", the speaker is what makes the meaning.
- Who is affected: Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers relying on captions, and viewers who cannot see who is talking (wide shots, a poster frame, low vision).
- Impact: Viewers may attribute "I think it should be scrapped" and "I disagree that it should be scrapped" to the wrong person, or lose track of the argument.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Keep the `<v>` tags, and also put a visible label in the cue text at each change of speaker, for example `<v Amara Okafor>AMARA OKAFOR: I think it should be scrapped.` and `<v Nils Lindqvist>NILS LINDQVIST: I disagree...`. Alternatively, position cues for each speaker consistently.
- Evidence: Cues at 00:03:12.000 and 00:04:40.000.
- Needs user testing: yes. Test caption rendering in the actual player and in browser fullscreen, with Deaf viewers.

### F7. The audio-described version is a bare MP4 link, probably without captions or controls context
- Where: lecture.html, line 125: `<a href="audio-described.mp4">Version with audio description</a>`
- Problem: The link goes to a raw MP4 file, not a page with a player. A file opened directly gets no `<track>`, so the audio-described version has no captions and no transcript beside it. The link does not say it opens or downloads a video file, or how large it is.
- Who is affected: Blind and partially sighted users, and deafblind users or users with both sight and hearing loss who need description and captions together. Users on metered data or with assistive technology that handles downloads poorly are also affected.
- Impact: Users who need both description and captions cannot have both. Some will get an unexpected download or a bare browser media view.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Link to a page, or a player option, that plays the described version with the same edited captions track (and a described transcript). Make the link text say what it opens, for example "Watch the version with audio description (opens video player page)". Alternatively, offer the audio-description track as a selectable option in the main player.
- Evidence: Line 125, and the absence of any track for the described file.
- Needs user testing: yes. Test with blind and deafblind users and with screen readers on mobile.

### F8. The site navigation is not marked up as navigation
- Where: lecture.html, line 116: `<header><p><a href="/">Harrow Vale University</a> · <a href="/lectures">Public lectures</a></p></header>`
- Problem: The breadcrumb trail is a paragraph with a "·" separator. It has no `<nav aria-label="Breadcrumb">`, no list and no `aria-current`. Screen readers may announce the separator as "dot" or "middle dot".
- Who is affected: Screen-reader users who move around by landmarks, and users with cognitive disabilities who rely on a clear sense of where they are.
- Impact: The navigation is harder to find and understand, and screen readers may add noise.
- Severity: 1
- Fix: Use `<nav aria-label="Breadcrumb"><ol><li><a href="/">Harrow Vale University</a></li><li><a href="/lectures">Public lectures</a></li></ol></nav>` and draw the separators with CSS.
- Evidence: Line 116.
- Needs user testing: no
