### F1. The audio-described version is a separate URL, so fetching it reveals the need
- Where: `lecture.html`, `<a href="audio-described.mp4">Version with audio description</a>`
- Problem: The only way to get audio description is a request for a distinct resource that only readers who want description will make. The path, the client IP, the User-Agent, the time and a same-origin Referer (`lecture.html`) reach the University's server or CDN, and any log or analytics behind it. A request for `audio-described.mp4` strongly suggests a blind or partially sighted reader, or someone helping one. If logged with an IP or a session or account cookie, that is likely special category data under UK GDPR (ASSUMED, not legal advice). Video playback also sends repeated range requests, which show that the reader kept watching the described version rather than just opening it.
- Who is affected: Blind and partially sighted readers, and readers with some cognitive or learning disabilities who use description.
- Impact: The publisher, or whoever runs its CDN and logs, can learn who needed what, which breaks principle 4. Nothing in the files shows whether these requests are logged, kept or linked to identity.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Do not make the need visible in the request. Options, from best to worst: (a) deliver description inside the same media request everyone makes, for example as an alternate audio rendition in one adaptive manifest that every player downloads, so choosing description happens on the device; or (b) add a small `kind="descriptions"` text track that every visit fetches by default, voiced on the device. Browser and screen-reader support for this is uneven and must be tested. If a separate file has to stay, (c) exclude its path from analytics, keep no per-IP or per-cookie access logs for it, set short retention, and add `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on the link. Option (c) reduces the leak but does not remove it.
- Evidence: The anchor `href="audio-described.mp4"` in `lecture.html`. The page shows no logging or analytics, so the logging risk is inferred from the request shape, not seen.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with blind and partially sighted readers whether (a) or (b) works in their own players and screen readers as well as a separate file does, and whether they would accept the separate file once (c) is in place.

### F2. The third-party video platform may receive caption and description choices as player events
- Where: The case says the AI summary "is made by the video platform" (StreamScribe). The `<video>` in `lecture.html` points to relative paths.
- Problem: If the media, captions or player are served or instrumented by StreamScribe, then events such as turning captions on or off, choosing a caption language, or starting the described version could go to a third party as telemetry. Caption-off combined with description-on, playback rate and similar choices together could form an identifying need profile (the composition leak). The files contain no third-party script, embed or beacon, so this cannot be confirmed from them.
- Who is affected: Deaf and hard-of-hearing readers (caption choices), blind and partially sighted readers (description), and readers who use slower playback.
- Impact: If it happens, need-revealing data goes to a third party. The University would likely be controller and StreamScribe its processor or a separate controller (ASSUMED, not legal advice). If it does not happen, there is no impact.
- Severity: 1 (the risk is real only if the platform is in the delivery path; raise to 2 if it is)
- Fix: Check how media is actually delivered and what the network sends during playback. Keep playback with the native `<video>` element and first-party files, as this page does. Send no player-state analytics, or only aggregate counts that are not tied to a client and do not carry caption or description choices. Write a no-telemetry term into the platform contract.
- Evidence: Inferred from the case text. `lecture.html` has no external script, iframe or tracking element.
- Needs disabled readers: no

Not found: no in-page settings panel, toggle, "screen reader mode", simplify, Easy Read, read-aloud, font or contrast control that could send a preference (size, contrast and motion are left to the browser and OS, which is the minimal design). No assistive-technology detection. No analytics, beacon, cookie or third-party script in the page. No form, account or declaration needed to get captions, the transcript or the described version, so no disclosure or purpose gate was seen. Captions (`default`) and the transcript (inline in `<details>`, opened on the device with no request) go to every reader, so they reveal no need. Whether the `/accessibility` page has an alternative-format request route that asks readers to disclose a disability cannot be checked, because that page is not in the files.
