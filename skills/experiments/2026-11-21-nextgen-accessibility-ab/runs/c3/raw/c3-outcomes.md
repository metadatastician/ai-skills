Reader tasks this material exists for: (T1) understand the proposal: where, when it operates, what is lost, what is gained and how sure the council is; (T2) respond to the consultation: state a position on the scale offered, comment (the full form specifically invites comment on the blue badge bays), and do it before the deadline; (T3) ask for another format or understand how the response will be used. Paths walked: full page (visual), full page through assistive-technology reading order, "Read aloud", "Simplify this page" (simplified.html), and the response record the council analyses, which is the outcome of T2 for the respondent.

### F1. Simplified version says "a lane just for buses" and drops the hours and the location
- Where: simplified.html, `<p>We want to make a lane just for buses on Riverside Road.</p>`; original consultation.html "What we propose", first paragraph.
- Problem: The original limits the lane to 7am to 10am and 4pm to 7pm on weekdays, open to all traffic otherwise, and between the Mill Street junction and the station. The simplified text drops both silently and, read plainly, states a bus-only lane at all times along Riverside Road. In outcome codes this is at least dropped (silent) for the hours and location, and arguably changed, because the sentence now asserts something the original does not.
- Who is affected: Readers who rely on the simplified view: those routed there by the button (labelled for learning disability), readers with low literacy, readers with cognitive fatigue, and readers new to English. They are the readers least likely to cross-check the full page.
- Impact: Performance: reader cannot tell when or where the lane applies. Safety: reader can be misinformed that driving or parking on Riverside Road would be barred all day, and may respond on that basis. Comfort: not an issue; the text is short.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Write a must-survive declaration before any adaptation; mark the operating hours, "open to all traffic outside those hours" and the start and end points as critical. Withhold any simplified version that drops or alters them. Produce the simple version from the original, check it against the declaration, and publish it only with an earned label.
- Evidence: Side-by-side text of consultation.html line "operating 7am to 10am and 4pm to 7pm on weekdays. Outside those hours the lane would be open to all traffic" against simplified.html. That such failures happen at material rates in AI adaptations generally is OPEN; this instance is observed directly in the case file.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test for a panel reading the simplified page only, compared with a panel reading the full page: "Could you drive a car in this lane at 1pm on a Tuesday?" "Could you drive in it on a Saturday?" "Which part of Riverside Road would have the lane?" Code each answer as correctly informed, uninformed (does not know) or misinformed (wrong answer given with confidence). People with learning disabilities should decide whether the hours can be kept in a form that stays simple.

### F2. Hedged model forecast turned into a certainty, and the cost to car journeys removed
- Where: simplified.html, `<p>Buses will be faster.</p>`; original "Our traffic modelling suggests bus journeys could be up to 4 minutes quicker at peak times. Car journeys on Riverside Road may be up to 2 minutes slower."
- Problem: The hedge ("modelling suggests", "could be up to") is changed into a flat promise. The number (up to 4 minutes, peak times only) is dropped. The stated downside (cars up to 2 minutes slower) is dropped silently, so the simplified page lists a benefit with no matching cost. Under the theory, a changed hedge is a distortion and makes the version Unfaithful whatever the weight.
- Who is affected: Simplified-view readers (as F1). Also anyone who uses the simplified page as a quick summary before responding.
- Impact: Performance: the reader cannot weigh the trade-off the consultation asks them to judge. Safety: misinformed about the certainty and the balance of effects; the omission of the cost tilts the material toward support, so the consultation outcome itself can be skewed. Comfort: not an issue.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Declare the hedge and both effects as critical (type: hedge or caveat; type: number). A faithful simple form keeps the uncertainty and both sides, for example that the council thinks buses may be a little quicker at busy times and cars may be a little slower; the wording should be set with Easy Read authors and checked against the declaration.
- Evidence: Text comparison of the two files. The general claim that AI answers distort content is TESTED for news (BBC 2025; EBU/BBC 2025); whether that transfers to this service is OPEN.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test: "Is the council sure buses will be faster?" "How much faster might buses be, and when?" "Would anything get slower?" Code correctly informed, uninformed or misinformed. Disabled readers should decide whether a hedge expressed in simple words counts as intact.

### F3. Blue badge bay move removed from the simple version, and the place to comment on it removed too
- Where: simplified.html, `<p>Some parking spaces will go.</p>` and the simplified form (no comments field); original paragraph 2 and the full-page label "Any comments on the proposal, including the blue badge bays".
- Problem: The original says twelve spaces go and four blue badge bays move 40 metres east to a layby outside number 62. The simple version drops the number, the side of the road, and the blue badge relocation entirely, without saying anything was cut. "Some parking spaces will go" could also be read as blue badge bays being removed, which the original does not say. The full form singles out the blue badge bays for comment; the simplified form has no comments field at all.
- Who is affected: Blue badge holders and their carers who use the simplified view. Disabled people who are directly affected by the change are the group the full page explicitly invites to comment, and the simplified path removes both the information and the channel.
- Impact: Performance: reader cannot find out whether their bay is affected, or where it moves to. Safety: may be misinformed that blue badge bays are going, or uninformed that they move; may plan around the wrong kerb. Comfort: a reader who needs the detail has to leave the simple view for the full page, losing the simplification they needed.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Declare the blue badge relocation (what, how far, where to) as critical. Keep it in every version. Carry the comments field, or an equally easy way to give a view (phone, paper, assisted response), into the simplified form.
- Evidence: consultation.html paragraph 2 and line 244 against simplified.html, which has no `textarea`.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test: "Will the blue badge bays on Riverside Road be removed, moved or left as they are?" "If moved, where to?" "Can you tell the council what you think about the blue badge bays on this page?" Blue badge holders, including those with learning disabilities, should set what must stay.

### F4. Simplified response form changes the question and collapses the scale, so positions are misrecorded
- Where: simplified.html form: legend "Do you like the bus lane idea?", two radios "Yes" (value `support`) and "No" (value `oppose`); full form: "Do you support the proposed bus lane?" with five options.
- Problem: The question is changed from support of a specific proposal to liking an "idea". The five-point scale becomes a forced binary with no neutral option. The values reuse `support` and `oppose`, so a simplified-view "Yes" is stored identically to a full-page "Support" (not "Strongly support"), merging two different answers into one code. Readers who are unsure, or support with conditions, have no way to say so and cannot add a comment.
- Who is affected: Everyone who responds through the simplified view; the council's analysis of all responses, because simplified-view answers are pooled with full-page answers under the same values.
- Impact: Performance: the reader cannot give their real position. Safety: their position is recorded as something they did not say (a neutral reader forced to "Yes" becomes "support"; strength of feeling is lost). This is a misrepresentation in the outcome of the consultation, not just the reading. Comfort: a two-choice form is lighter, but the lightness is bought by removing options.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Keep the same question and the same scale in every version, worded simply and with a neutral and an "I don't know" option, and a comments field. If a simpler response format is used, store it with distinct values and report it as a different instrument rather than merging it.
- Evidence: simplified.html lines for the fieldset; server-notes.md says responses are exported "for analysis", with no mention of reconciling the two instruments.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test (with consent, after the respondent submits): "Which of these is closest to what you meant to tell the council?" (five options plus "not sure"), compared with what was stored. Code agreement, under-statement, over-statement or wrong direction. People with learning disabilities should decide what a simple but faithful response scale looks like.

### F5. Simplified version is unlabelled, made from an unchecked prompt, and cached for a week
- Where: server-notes.md ("Rewrite for a reader with <need>", "caches the result for a week"); simplified.html has no label, no source, no date, and no statement that it was machine-made.
- Problem: No must-survive declaration exists, so no version can be Checked. The page carries no label, no "made by AI from the page dated X" record, and does not tell the reader that facts were cut. The only route back is a "See the full page" link after the form, so a reader submits before reaching it. A week-long cache means that if the full page is corrected, the simplified version keeps serving the old text (a chain fault).
- Who is affected: Simplified-view readers, who have no way of knowing they are reading an unchecked machine version; also any reader who reached the page during a correction window.
- Impact: Performance: reader may treat the simple page as complete. Safety: no signal to cross-check the facts at F1 to F3; risk of acting on a stale version after a correction. Comfort: n/a.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Add a label near the top (Unchecked until a check earns Checked; Withheld if a critical item cannot survive), naming who made it, from which version of the original and when. Invalidate the cache whenever the original changes. Put "See the full page" before the form, and say what the simple page leaves out. Whether labels improve reader outcomes is OPEN, so test it.
- Evidence: server-notes.md bullet 2; simplified.html contents. Label efficacy: OPEN.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test, with and without a label: "Does this page tell you everything the council is proposing?" "Who wrote this simple page?" "Where could you find more detail?" Disabled readers should say whether a label is understood and helpful or adds load.

### F6. Using the simple view tags the respondent as having a learning disability, and that tag travels with their response
- Where: consultation.html link `?view=simple&needs=learning-disability`; script copying `needs` into hidden `a11y_profile`; simplified.html hidden `a11y_profile` value `learning-disability`; server-notes.md (vendor prompt, 90-day log of IP and query string, response stored with `a11y_profile`, spreadsheet breakdown by `a11y_profile`); Read aloud beacon with `uid`.
- Problem: Pressing "Simplify this page" records the reader as having a learning disability, whatever their reason for wanting simpler text. That inferred disability is sent to a vendor, logged against IP for 90 days, stored with the person's consultation response and broken out in analysis. Read aloud use is logged against a persistent `uid`. Principle 4 says the publisher never learns who needed what; here it learns, and records a disability that may be wrong.
- Who is affected: Every simplified-view user (including people with no disability, mislabelled), every read-aloud user, and especially readers who notice the URL and decide not to use the simple view or not to respond.
- Impact: Performance: a reader who avoids the simple view to protect their privacy loses the adaptation they need, and may be uninformed or not respond. Safety: responses can be read or weighted differently by profile; a wrong disability record is kept as special category data (legal points ASSUMED). Comfort: the reader has to trade privacy against access.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Remove `needs` from the URL, the prompt, the logs, the hidden fields and the analytics. Do simplification on the reader's side or from a pre-made, checked version served without any profile. Remove the `uid` beacon. If uptake is needed, count anonymous totals that cannot be linked to a response.
- Evidence: consultation.html lines 223, 235, 251 to 260; simplified.html hidden fields; server-notes.md bullets 2 to 4.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test: compare response completion and comprehension (F1 to F3 questions) between a version that tags and one that does not; ask "Before using this button, did you know the council would record it with your answer?" Disabled readers should decide whether any uptake measure is acceptable.

### F7. Read aloud reads the whole main region, including the form, with no stop or pace control, and is absent from the simple view
- Where: consultation.html script, `speechSynthesis.speak(new SpeechSynthesisUtterance(document.querySelector('main').innerText))`; simplified.html has no Read aloud button.
- Problem: The audio path is Exact for the proposal text (form only), which is good for substance. But it also reads the tool links, every radio label and the submit text as a run of words with no selection state, and offers no pause, stop, replay or speed control; a second click queues another full reading. Readers who need both simpler text and audio cannot have both.
- Who is affected: Readers who rely on listening (low vision without a screen reader, dyslexia, cognitive fatigue), and those who need both simple text and audio.
- Impact: Performance: the facts arrive intact, but the reader must hold them in memory with no way to go back. Safety: low; the substance is not changed. Comfort: undue effort and time; no way to stop it short of leaving the page.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Give pause, stop, replay by section and speed controls; read the proposal sections, not the form; offer the same audio on any simplified version, reading the checked version only.
- Evidence: script lines 256 to 261; absence of any control in simplified.html.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test after listening only: the F1 and F2 questions (hours, location, certainty, cost), plus "Did you have to listen to anything more than once, and could you?" Listening readers should judge whether the effort is acceptable.

### F8. No route to ask for another format, and the simple page drops the accessibility statement, privacy notice and the year
- Where: consultation.html (footer links only); simplified.html (no footer; "Tell us what you think by 15 December").
- Problem: Neither page says how to get Easy Read, paper, large print, a phone or in-person response, or help filling in the form. The simple page removes the accessibility statement and privacy notice links, so its readers cannot find out how their response (and the profile in F6) is used. The deadline keeps the day but drops the year and the opening date; "by 15 December" against "open to 15 December" leaves it unclear whether the 15th itself counts.
- Who is affected: Readers for whom neither web version works; simplified-view readers in particular, who also cannot reach the privacy notice.
- Impact: Performance: a reader who needs another format has no stated way to ask, and may miss the consultation. Safety: possible misreading of the closing date (minor). Comfort: a reader must search the site to find help.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: State on both pages how to ask for other formats and assisted responses, with a contact that does not require disclosing a diagnosis. Keep the privacy notice and accessibility statement links on the simple page. State the closing date and time in full in every version (declare it critical, type: instruction).
- Evidence: footer of consultation.html; absence of footer in simplified.html.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Outcome test: "If this page did not work for you, how would you ask the council for it another way?" "What is the last day you can send your view?" Code correctly informed, uninformed or misinformed.
