### F1. The badge and conformance claim present an automated score as full conformance with a version of WCAG that does not exist
- Where: `<span class="badge">WCAG 2.3 AAA verified</span>`; first paragraph under "How accessible this website is" ("fully conformant with ... version 2.3 at Level AAA", "scores 100 out of 100 in automated accessibility audits", "you can be confident every page meets the standard").
- Problem: There is no WCAG 2.3. The current recommendation is WCAG 2.2. An automated audit checks only a subset of success criteria and cannot establish conformance, let alone full AAA. The page treats the machine-checkable slice as the whole thing and turns it into a "verified" badge. Nothing links the badge to evidence. To a screen reader it is plain text that is announced as a fact, and a sighted reader sees it styled like a certification mark.
- Who is affected: Every tenant who relies on the statement to decide whether the site will work for them. This weighs most on people who meet barriers that automated tools cannot detect: cognitive access, reading level, timing, and the quality of captions and descriptions. They are told those barriers do not exist, and they may blame themselves or give up rather than report a problem.
- Impact: Readers are misled about what to expect. A reader who meets a barrier is given no reason to report it, because the statement says nothing is wrong.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Remove the badge. State conformance against WCAG 2.2, and say whether the site is partially or fully conformant, based on testing that includes manual testing and testing with disabled people. Report the automated score, if at all, as what it is: "an automated tool found no issues of the kinds it can detect; automated tools detect only some barriers."
- Evidence: Theory section "Version and claim honesty" (no WCAG 2.3; an analyser cannot establish conformance or "full AAA"). Section "The alignment gap": "A high automated score is not conformance and must not be read as it", and in GDS's 2017 test the best automated tool found 41% of 143 planted barriers.
- Needs disabled readers: no. The version and scope errors are factual.

### F2. "Testing" names only an automated tool, so the barriers that only people meet were never looked for
- Where: The "Testing" section ("last tested on 1 November 2026 by AccessGuard AI, an automated tool. All issues it found have been fixed.") and "checked every night by our AccessGuard AI".
- Problem: The only evidence offered is the automated slice. An automated tool cannot measure caption or BSL accuracy, reading level, cognitive load, timing or whether alternatives are faithful. "All issues it found have been fixed" is true only of what the tool can find, but the page reads it as covering everything. The two statements also do not agree: one says the site is checked every night, the other says it was last tested on 1 November.
- Who is affected: People whose barriers fall outside automated detection: users with cognitive impairments, learning disabilities or dyslexia, people who rely on captions, BSL or Easy Read, and people who need more time. Whether these barriers are the commonest, rather than merely the least detected, is DESIGNED and not tested.
- Impact: No known-issues list exists, so a reader cannot tell which parts of the site to avoid or which parts to ask for in another format.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Add manual and assistive-technology testing, and user testing with disabled tenants, with dates and scope. Publish a list of known non-accessible content and say how each item can be reached another way. Make the testing dates agree with each other.
- Evidence: The theory says automated tools "cannot count caption accuracy, reading level, cognitive load or timing at all". The case material shows no manual testing and no known-issues section. The claim that these barriers block people but not agents is DESIGNED.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test whether tenants with learning disabilities, dyslexia or cognitive impairments can find and understand key tenancy pages. That cannot be settled from the markup.

### F3. AI-generated Easy Read and BSL are promised as "instantly and accurately", with no label and no check
- Where: "Our AI assistant can rewrite any page in Easy Read, British Sign Language video or another language on request, instantly and accurately."
- Problem: Instant machine output is Unchecked by default. A tool's claim about itself is not evidence, and a version cannot award itself a label. The page offers no label, no must-survive declaration, no record of who made a version, from what or when, and no Withheld route. "Accurately" claims a fidelity that has not been measured. The page also gives no way to reach this assistant, so the claim cannot be acted on and cannot be checked.
- Who is affected: Easy Read readers (people with learning disabilities, some cognitive-access users), Deaf BSL users and readers of translations. These are the readers least able to compare a version with the English original. Tenancy matters carry critical items such as amounts, deadlines, obligations and rights of reply, where a silent drop or change does real harm.
- Impact: A reader may act on an unfaithful version of a tenancy letter or notice and believe it is accurate. Whether AI accessibility adaptations break declared items at material rates is OPEN, and the claim of accuracy rests on nothing.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Remove "accurately". Label each AI version (Exact, Checked, Unchecked or Withheld) and record its source and date. Make every version from the current original. Withhold versions of documents with critical items, such as notices, rent figures and legal deadlines, unless they have been checked. Offer human-made Easy Read and BSL as the route for those documents. Mark machine-made versions as the EU AI Act Article 50 requires, and add the label as well, because the mark says only that a machine was involved.
- Evidence: Theory sections "Labels for a version" and "Outcome codes". The evidence-status table lists "AI accessibility adaptations break declared items at material rates" as OPEN, and "AI answers about news often distort content" as TESTED, in a different domain.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Deaf BSL users need to judge whether machine BSL video is intelligible and intact, and Easy Read readers, with their supporters, need to judge whether a version is intact (principle 5). Neither can be settled by a tool or by this reviewer.

### F4. The form requires disability disclosure as an eligibility gate, and the markup is technically flawless
- Where: The `#disability` select (`required`, hint "We need this to check that you are eligible for an alternative format."), and the paragraph after the submit button: "Information you give us about your disability is shared with your housing officer and kept on your tenancy file."
- Problem: The disability field passes automated checks. It has an explicit label, a programmatic hint through `aria-describedby` and a required state. Still, it makes a reader disclose special category data before they can get the format they need. The format field alone is enough to meet the request. The single-choice list forces a reader with more than one condition, or one not listed, into one category. "Other" opens no text field. The note that the answer goes to the housing officer and onto the tenancy file comes after the submit button. A reader who goes through the form in order, including a screen reader user moving between fields in forms mode, may reach that note only after they have sent the request, or never.
- Who is affected: Any disabled tenant who asks for another format, especially people with mental health conditions or learning disabilities, who may fear that disclosure will affect their tenancy. Also anyone who needs a format but would not describe themselves in these terms, such as an older tenant with low vision.
- Impact: Some readers may not ask at all, which leaves the barrier in place. Those who do ask have their needs revealed to the publisher and stored against their tenancy. The automated score records none of this.
- Severity: 3
- Fix: Remove the disability field. Ask only for the format and the document. If any optional information is kept, state clearly before the form what is collected, who sees it, why and for how long, and do not put it on the tenancy file by default. Add an "Other format" option with free text instead of a disability category.
- Evidence: Theory section "Reader privacy and personal adaptation" ("No request ... may reveal a reader's needs"; disability information is special category data under UK GDPR) and principle 4 ("the publisher never learns who needed what"). The legal points are ASSUMED and await counsel's opinion. The markup is in the case file.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test with tenants, including people with mental health conditions and learning disabilities, whether the disclosure requirement and the after-button privacy note deter requests or are understood before submission.

### F5. The request route depends on the inaccessible material it replaces, and the options and timing are narrow
- Where: The request form, the "Contact" section (email only) and "we will reply within 10 working days".
- Problem: The only routes are a web form and an email address. There is no phone, text relay, BSL video relay or postal route. The format list has no plain-language, electronic accessible document (such as tagged PDF or Word), coloured-paper or "other" option. Easy Read and BSL appear here, while the page also says AI produces them "instantly". The reader cannot tell whether a request gets a human-made version or AI output, which F3 makes material. A 10-working-day wait can outlast deadlines in the letter being requested.
- Who is affected: People who cannot use the form or email unaided (some blind, cognitive-access, switch and voice-control users), Deaf BSL users, and readers waiting on a letter with a deadline.
- Impact: A tenant may miss a rent, repair or legal deadline while waiting for a format they cannot otherwise read. An automated tool does not detect any of this.
- Severity: 2
- Fix: Add phone, text relay, BSL video relay and postal routes. Add an "Other format" option with free text. Say whether each format is made or checked by a person. Commit that deadlines in a requested document will be extended, or that urgent items will be handled faster.
- Evidence: Case file form and contact section. The theory says automated tools cannot count timing or cognitive load.
- Needs disabled readers: yes. Test whether tenants who use switch access, voice control or a screen reader, and Deaf BSL users, can complete a request through the offered routes, and whether the list covers the formats they need.

### F6. A fix aimed at agents or scores would add noise for people, and agent-filled forms would leak needs
- Where: The badge, the claims paragraph and the form, considered as targets for "AI readiness" work.
- Problem: The current markup is lean. It has no stray ARIA, no hidden agent text and no live regions, and the visible label and announced name match. The risk is in what the page invites. A publisher chasing an automated score or agent parsing could add `aria-label`s, structured "accessibility" metadata or hidden badge text that restates the claim, and screen reader users would hear it twice. The reverse case: an agent working for a tenant, as personal adaptation, cannot get past the required disability field without inventing an answer or revealing one. A "fix" that lets agents pre-fill disability from a stored profile would turn reader-side adaptation into a disclosure channel, which is the composition leak.
- Who is affected: Screen reader users, through duplicated or verbose announcements. Tenants who use assistive agents, through disclosure.
- Impact: The harm here is prospective, not present in this file. WebAIM's ARIA error figures are a correlation, not a cause.
- Severity: 1
- Fix: Do not add agent-directed markup or metadata to support the claims. Fix F1 and F4 at source by removing the claim and the disability field, rather than making either more machine-readable.
- Evidence: Theory section "The alignment gap" (59.1 errors against 42, correlation, not cause) and "Reader privacy" (composition leak; personal adaptation on the reader's own device is always permitted).
- Needs disabled readers: no.

Not found: no structured data (JSON-LD or microdata), hidden or off-screen text for agents or search, keyword-stuffed or verbose accessible names, ARIA added for "AI readiness", live regions, or generated alt text in this file. There are no images, media, captions, transcripts or motion on the page to assess, and no plain-language mismatch between visible and announced text: labels and hints are announced as shown. Apart from the disability field forcing an agent to invent or reveal an answer (F6), I found no present barrier that blocks agents but not people.
