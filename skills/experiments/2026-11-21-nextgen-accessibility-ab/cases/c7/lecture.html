Full transcript

Ruth Hale (chair): Welcome. Tonight's question is whether the four-day school week, now in its second year in twelve pilot districts, should be scrapped. Amara, you first.

Amara Okafor: I think it should be scrapped. In the pilot districts, reading scores for the youngest pupils are down by about 12 per cent, and that's on top of the gap we already had. And Friday childcare has fallen on parents, mostly mothers.

Nils Lindqvist: I disagree that it should be scrapped. I accept the reading figures for the youngest pupils. But maths scores are flat, attendance is up, and teacher vacancies in those districts have halved. My view is: reform it. Keep the four-day week for older pupils, and fund Friday provision for the youngest.

Amara Okafor: If the Friday provision is funded properly, I could live with that for secondary schools. I'm not there for primary.

Ruth Hale (chair): So, not agreement, but some common ground on secondary schools. The evaluation report is due next spring, and the minister has said nothing will change before it is published. Thank you both.
