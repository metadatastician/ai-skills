Council approves £4.2m flood barrier for Lower Wendham

By Priya Nair, Local Democracy Reporter · Published 12 November 2026, updated 14 November 2026

Key points

  • A £4.8m flood barrier will protect 900 homes in Lower Wendham.
  • Councillor Ana Ruiz said the scheme had been rushed through without consultation.
  • Work starts in spring and the barrier will be finished by 2028.

Wendham District Council's cabinet has approved a £4.2m flood barrier along the River Wend, after two winters of flooding in Lower Wendham.

Cabinet member for environment, Councillor Ana Ruiz, said:

"We expect this to cut the flood risk for around 900 homes, though no barrier can remove the risk entirely."

The Environment Agency said the barrier "could reduce" the chance of flooding in a severe winter, but that its modelling was still being reviewed.

Not everyone is convinced. Malik Osei, chair of the Lower Wendham Residents' Association, said:

"This has been rushed through without proper consultation. Residents on Mill Lane will lose their river access and nobody asked them."

The council said a public consultation on the barrier's design would open in January, before construction begins.

Work is expected to start in spring 2027, subject to the consultation, with completion planned for late 2028.

Hallam Civils, the contractor named in the council papers, said it would respond to residents' concerns about river access "in due course".

Correction, 14 November 2026: an earlier version of this article said the scheme would cost £4.8m. The approved cost is £4.2m.
